# Build CropStack's catalog from facts, not mirrors

CropStack can build an accurate, worldwide catalog legally, but almost none of it can come from one clean, bulk-downloadable, openly licensed dataset. The bundleable core is small: Wikidata (CC0) and World Flora Online snapshots (CC0) for names; USDA PLANTS (CC0) and GRIN (US public domain) for US-sourced traits; FAO ECOCROP and FAO DAD-IS (both CC BY 4.0 plus FAO's extra terms); pyfao56's machine-readable FAO-56 crop coefficients (CC0 code, with the FAO numbers cited as facts); the BBCH growth-stage scale (CC BY 4.0); DSSAT crop-model parameters (BSD-3); EPPO Codes (open licence); the OpenFarm rescue set (CC0, 340 crops); Wikipedia's companion list (CC BY-SA 4.0); GloBI interactions; and GBIF, iNaturalist and Commons photos filtered per item. The best practical references cannot be bundled at all. That group includes extension guides, ARC-VOPI, UC IPM, CABI, PFAF, Feedipedia, HortNZ, FERTASA and the varroa guide. They carry all-rights-reserved, non-commercial or no-derivatives terms. CropStack may store individual numeric facts from them with a citation, but must never copy their prose, tables or images. Two licence conflicts in the earlier notes are now settled. **ECOCROP is CC BY 4.0**, as its own FAO Data Catalog record states. **OpenFarm data is CC0**, as is the 340-record rescue repository. The recommended setup has four parts. Keep MIT for code and put `catalog/` under **CC BY-SA 4.0**. Attach a per-value provenance record that cites a central source registry. Run a maintainer-only, API-first ingestion pipeline whose output lands as reviewed pull requests. Self-hosted instances should never scrape. Several areas still have no open source of any kind: home-garden yield per m², seed longevity, square-foot spacing counts, frost-kill and vernalization tables, vegetable nutrient uptake by growth stage, South African pesticide and stock-remedy registers, and withdrawal periods. Those must be curated by hand from cited facts or left to user input.

## Facts are free, compilations and prose are not

The legal basis for the whole design is the line between facts and expression. In the US, Feist held that "no author may copyright his ideas or the facts he narrates" and that copyright in a factual compilation is "thin" ([Justia, 499 U.S. 340](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/499/340/)). The US Copyright Office adds that a list of ingredients or simple directions is not protectable, but a creative written explanation is ([USCO Circular 33](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf)). US federal works have no copyright protection at all ([17 USC 105](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/105)). Three other jurisdictions are stricter. In the EU, the database right protects investment in *obtaining, verifying or presenting* data, and repeated extraction of small parts is caught when together they reconstitute a substantial part ([CJEU, BHB v William Hill](https://curia.europa.eu/en/actu/communiques/cp04/aff/cp040089en.pdf)). For unprotected databases, Ryanair v PR Aviation lets site owners ban scraping by contract ([SCL](https://www.scl.org/3280-the-cjeu-takes-flight-databases-and-air-fares/)). The UK keeps its own database right after Brexit ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/sui-generis-database-rights)). **South Africa matters most for the maintainer.** Its Copyright Act treats "tables and compilations of data" as literary works, and its originality bar rewards skill and labour (*Haupt v Brewers*), so even plain compilations can be protected, although there is no separate database right ([CIPIT/Strathmore](https://cipit.strathmore.edu/database-protection-and-the-limits-of-copyright-lessons-from-south-africa/)). The Copyright Amendment Bill's fair-use provisions were upheld in June 2026, but parts were struck down and the Bill cannot yet be signed. It has no text-and-data-mining exception ([GoLegal](https://www.golegal.co.za/constitutional-court-copyright/)).

The working rules follow from this. Taking individual values (6 mm deep, germinates at 20–30 °C, 70 days to maturity) from many sources, each cited, is safe everywhere. Copying or closely paraphrasing care prose, symptom text or images is not. Neither is reproducing one site's whole species list together with its field set. That second risk is highest for South African compilations such as ARC tables and for EU/UK databases, because GitHub redistribution reaches the EU and UK even though the maintainer is in South Africa. This is not legal advice. The notes found no South African case law on scraping plant-care data, and a local IP lawyer should confirm the *Haupt* risk.

Licences then decide what can be **bundled** in the repo, as opposed to cited. Creative Commons licences "are operative only when applied to material in which a copyright exists" ([CC FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/)), so reading an NC source to check a number does not breach it. NC and ND material cannot be bundled, though. MIT allows commercial use, self-hosters may be farms, and NC content cannot be relicensed. The table below sets out the verdict for every source the notes assessed. "Bundle" means values may ship in `catalog/` under CC BY-SA 4.0 with attribution. "Facts only" means individual cited values, with no prose, tables or images. "Link" means a reference URL only.

## Two licence conflicts resolved in CropStack's favour

**ECOCROP is CC BY 4.0. This supersedes the earlier notes that attached CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO or FAO's publication rules to it.** The FAO Data Catalog record `ecocrop` carries `license_id: "CC-BY-4.0"` with `isopen: true`. It was modified on 3 April 2025 and is maintained by the FAO Land and Water Division ([FAO Data Catalog API](https://data.apps.fao.org/catalog/api/3/action/package_show?id=ecocrop)). The confusion came from mixing two FAO regimes. CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO and the "no CC licence before June 2018" rule belong to the open-access policy for FAO **publications** ([FAO Open Access policy](https://openknowledge.fao.org/bitstreams/6e7af061-3928-4c55-99c5-6b5e4cb8e3f0/download)). Datasets fall under the Statistical Database Terms, which default to CC BY 4.0 "unless specified otherwise in their metadata or webpage" ([FAO DB terms](https://www.fao.org/contact-us/terms/db-terms-of-use/en/)). ECOCROP does not appear in that page's Annex 1, but the Annex is titled "examples", and ECOCROP's own metadata explicitly says CC BY 4.0. There are two practical catches. First, FAO adds terms outside the CC licence: no use to promote a commercial enterprise and no implied endorsement. These cannot be passed on through BY-SA, so they belong in a NOTICE file for the ECOCROP subset rather than being treated as void. Second, the catalog record links only to the interactive tool, so **there is no official bulk download**. The only bulk copy is the R package Recocrop. Its code is GPL ≥3 and it holds 1,710 taxa whose values are partly transformed: monthly rainfall is derived from annual rainfall, and duration is set to GMIN+1 month ([CRAN Recocrop](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/Recocrop/index.html)). CropStack should attribute values to FAO ECOCROP, use only Recocrop's untransformed raw `ecocrop.rds` fields or spot-check them against the live tool, and never present Recocrop's derived columns as FAO originals. Coverage figures disagree across sources: 2,568 species on FAO's page, "more than 2000" in the catalog and 1,710 in Recocrop ([FAO Geospatial](https://www.fao.org/geospatial/data-and-tools/data-portals/ecocrop/en)). The ecocrop.apps.fao.org footer could not be read because the page is rendered in JavaScript, and whether FAO considers scraping the tool acceptable is unverified.

**OpenFarm data is CC0, and so is the 340-record rescue repo. This supersedes the earlier notes that said the data licence was unconfirmed or CC BY.** The archived OpenFarm README has a "Data License" section stating "All data within the OpenFarm.cc database is in the Public Domain (CC0)". The MIT LICENSE file covers only the code ([OpenFarm README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openfarmcc/OpenFarm/mainline/README.md)). OpenFarm's servers shut down on 21 April 2025, and no official dump was ever released ([FarmBot blog](https://farm.bot/blogs/news/sunsetting-openfarm)). The community repo `thefullnacho/openfarm-crops-rescue` is CC0 1.0. It rebuilt **340 crop records** from Wayback captures of 358 pages, and every record carries `source.license: "CC0-1.0"` and a `waybackUrl`. Field coverage is uneven: 330 records have descriptions, 305 have binomials and only 134 have companion lists. Its author warns: "Treat this as a useful scaffold, not an authority" ([rescue repo](https://github.com/thefullnacho/openfarm-crops-rescue)). Use it as a seed list of crop slugs, spacing and sowing methods, with every value marked `evidence: grower-reported` until it is checked.

Three related verifications support the rest of the plan. **FAO DAD-IS is listed in Annex 1** and so defaults to CC BY 4.0, with the citation format "FAO. [year]. [Database]. Accessed on [date]. [URL] Licence: CC-BY-4.0." ([FAO DB terms](https://www.fao.org/contact-us/terms/db-terms-of-use/en/)). Whether the DAD-IS site overrides that default in its own metadata was not checked. **pyfao56 is CC0, but its `tables.py` makes no claim about FAO's rights in the FAO-56 tables** ([tables.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kthorp/pyfao56/main/src/pyfao56/tools/tables.py)). FAO-56 itself is "© FAO 1998" with all rights reserved ([FAO-56](https://www.fao.org/4/x0490e/x0490e00.htm)). So store the bare Kc, stage-length, root-depth and depletion values under CropStack's own crop keys, cite "Allen et al. 1998, FAO-56 Table N" for each, and do not copy the table layout or footnotes. **Wikipedia's companion list is CC BY-SA 4.0**, with about 99 plant rows ([raw wikitext](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=List_of_companion_plants&action=raw)). Pin a revision ID when importing it, because it could not be captured during research.

## Each catalog domain has a different open backbone

**Crops, varieties and names.** Wikidata is the crosswalk. It holds 9,879 cultivar items and 2,636 Afrikaans common names as of 9 October 2026, plus external IDs that link GBIF, USDA, GRIN and WFO ([Wikidata:Licensing](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Licensing)). Accepted names should come from the CC0 WFO backbone. The newest WFO releases could not be confirmed as still CC0, so pin a verified Zenodo snapshot. SANBI's checklist covers South African natives. Vegetable cultivars are the weak point, because no open source lists them comprehensively. Seed catalogs and GRIN accession names have to be curated, and NPGS germplasm itself is "not available for individual, home, or community gardening" ([GRIN](https://npgsweb.ars-grin.gov/gringlobal/search)).

**Requirement profiles.** ECOCROP supplies temperature, rainfall and pH ranges, killing temperature and growing season. USDA PLANTS adds frost-free days, minimum temperature, pH, rainfall and root depth through an undocumented JSON API. It is rich for cover crops but empty for tomato: the research test returned 390 characteristic rows for crimson clover and 0 for tomato ([data.gov PLANTS](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/plants)). Its values are in imperial units and the data was last modified in 2022, so snapshot it rather than calling it at runtime.

**Growing parameters and phenology.** pyfao56 gives FAO-56 Tables 11, 12, 17 and 22, which hold stage lengths, Kc and Kcb, maximum root depth and depletion fraction for most garden vegetables. These are stratified by climate and planting month, which suits a location-aware planner ([pyfao56](https://github.com/kthorp/pyfao56)). The BBCH monograph (CC BY 4.0) provides the stage vocabulary for about 26 vegetables ([BBCH](https://www.openagrar.de/servlets/MCRFileNodeServlet/openagrar_derivate_00016780/BBCH%20ENGLISCH_%20.pdf)). DSSAT's BSD-3 genotype files give cardinal temperatures and thermal-time targets for tomato, pepper, cabbage, green bean and potato ([DSSAT Genotype](https://github.com/DSSAT/dssat-csm-os/tree/develop/Data/Genotype)). Label these as model-calibration values, not gardener rules. Germination minimum, optimum and maximum temperatures and days to emergence for about 24 vegetables come from the Harrington tables, cited as facts via OSU ([OSU](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/es/catalog/soil-temperature-conditions-vegetable-seed-germination)). Storage life for 138 commodities comes from USDA AH-66. Stone-fruit chill requirements for about 530 Prunus cultivars come from Fadón et al. 2020 ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4395/10/3/409)), whose CC licence was only partly verified. For South African sowing months, ARC-VOPI guidelines are the authority, but ARC's terms forbid reproducing or adapting them without written permission ([ARC terms](https://www.arc.agric.za/Pages/Terms-and-Conditions.aspx)). A maintainer based in South Africa is well placed to request that permission.

**Pests, diseases and beneficials.** Only EPPO Codes are openly licensed among the authoritative plant-health references. Their licence permits commercial use and requires the notice "Contains EPPO Codes (www.eppo.int)" and the download date ([EPPO licence](https://data.eppo.int/documentation/opendata)). EPPO host and distribution content needs a token and has no stated open licence, and its photos are for "educational purposes only". GloBI supplies pathogen-host, parasitoid-host and pollinator links as versioned Zenodo dumps, but licensing follows each source dataset, so rows must be filtered ([GloBI](https://globalbioticinteractions.org/data)). Photos come from GBIF, iNaturalist and Commons, filtered per image. For iNaturalist, query with `photo_license=cc0,cc-by` or use the AWS open-data bucket, because the default licence is CC BY-NC and an observation's photos may be licensed differently from the observation itself ([iNat open data](https://github.com/inaturalist/inaturalist-open-data)). Under a CC BY-SA 4.0 catalog, BY-SA photos are also compatible. The earlier note that excluded them assumed a CC BY or MIT data licence. Weather-driven disease models can be written from their published rules. The Hutton criteria are two consecutive days with a minimum of 10 °C and at least 6 hours at RH ≥90% ([AHDB](https://potatoes.ahdb.org.uk/development-and-implementation-of-a-new-national-warning-system-for-potato-late-blight-in-great-britain-hutton-criteria)). The Smith Period and the Gubler-Thomas powdery-mildew index can be implemented the same way. Re-implement them rather than copying NIBIO's AGPL-derived code. Degree-day thresholds from UC IPM and USPest.org are facts, but they are calibrated for the US. Flag them as unvalidated in the Southern Hemisphere and shift the biofix by season. For vision diagnosis, PlantDoc and DeepWeeds are CC BY 4.0. PlantVillage's licence is contradictory across sources, so treat it as non-commercial ([PlantDoc](https://github.com/pratikkayal/PlantDoc-Dataset); [DeepWeeds](https://zenodo.org/record/7939059)).

**Companion planting.** The Wikipedia table is the only open, ready-made pair list, and the page itself admits "only a few of these have been subjected to scientific testing" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_companion_plants)). The peer-reviewed evidence supports the general principle, not the folk pairs. A 2026 meta-analysis of 449 publications found that intercropping cut herbivores by **39%** and crop damage by **30%**, and raised predators by 48% ([Seimandi-Corda et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12946007/)). Diversification's mean yield effect was small but negative ([Letourneau et al. 2011](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1890/09-2026.1)). Pair-specific support exists for a few cases: French marigold slowing whitefly on tomato ([Conboy et al. 2019](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0213071)), the Three Sisters' land-equivalent yield gain ([Zhang et al. 2014](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1093/aob/mcu191)), and named trap crops such as Indian mustard for diamondback moth ([Sarkar et al. 2018](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4450/9/4/128)). For CropStack to stay truthful, every imported pair should default to `evidence: traditional` and carry a mechanism tag (trap, repel, natural-enemy habitat, complementarity or allelopathy). A pair should be promoted only when a pair-specific study is cited. Untested claims such as "carrots love tomatoes" are unverified, not disproven.

**Rotation.** Family groupings are public taxonomic fact. Store the APG IV family (Amaryllidaceae, Amaranthaceae and so on) plus a garden "rotation group" alias, following Cornell's 3-year family rotation ([CCE](https://allegany.cce.cornell.edu/gardening/food-gardening/rotating-vegetables-by-family)). Disease-specific overrides should be cited facts and should cross family lines, because Sclerotinia and Verticillium hosts span families:

**Nutrients and yields.** No open-licence table of vegetable N-P-K uptake or removal was found. Values must come from FAO Bulletin 16 (non-commercial reproduction only), AHDB RB209 offtakes via the PDA (for example potato P₂O₅ 1.0 and K₂O 5.8 kg/t), CDFA/UCCE nitrogen-removal coefficients, and HortNZ, stored as cited numbers ([FAO](https://www.fao.org/4/a0443e/a0443e.pdf); [PDA](https://www.pda.org.uk/?p=509); [CDFA FREP](https://blogs.cdfa.ca.gov/FREP/index.php/research-update-estimating-nitrogen-removal-from-the-harvested-portion-of-central-coast-crops)). Model feeding as removal per tonne multiplied by expected yield. Yield is the biggest gap. The only open lead for home-garden data is Sheffield's Measure Your Harvest dataset, whose licence was not confirmed ([ORDA](https://orda.shef.ac.uk/articles/dataset/Measure_Your_Harvest_long-term_citizen-science_reveals_the_contribution_of_own-growing_to_UK_food_security_-_dataset/30675815)). Until then, convert commercial t/ha figures (1 t/ha = 0.1 kg/m²) and label them `commercial benchmark`.

**Livestock and bees.** DAD-IS (CC BY 4.0) is the breed backbone, with more than 8,800 breeds across about 40 species and 182 countries, plus bee data. It offers web export only and no documented API ([DAD-IS](https://www.fao.org/dad-is/en/)). Import only the structured fields, because coordinator-uploaded text and photos may be third-party content. Wikidata adds multilingual breed labels, but it has no DAD-IS ID property, so the two must be matched on name, species and country. Husbandry numbers come from cited sources. The laying-hen figures (at most 9 hens/m², 1 nest per 7 hens, 15 cm of perch) come from EU Directive 1999/74/EC ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:31999L0074)). The dairy THI formula and its 72 comfort threshold come from an open-access CC BY review ([Mbuthia et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/animal-science/articles/10.3389/fanim.2022.946592/pdf)). Incubation and gestation periods come from the MSD Vet Manual, cited as facts only ([MSD](https://www.msdvetmanual.com/en-au/exotic-and-laboratory-animals/backyard-poultry/laying-and-reproduction-in-backyard-poultry)). Varroa action thresholds of 1% and 2% come from the CC BY-NC-ND HBHC guide, cited and linked but not bundled. For South African beekeepers, show a warning about the capensis–scutellata boundary ([ARC-PPRI](https://arc.agric.za/arc-ppri/Pages/Insect%20Ecology/Honeybee-Biology.aspx)). Withdrawal periods should not ship at all. FARAD states no reuse licence ([FARAD](https://farad.org/)), and no open South African register exists. Let users enter the withdrawal days from the product label, and calculate the safe date from that.

## Gaps with no open source

Some needs have no openly licensed source, and the catalog should say so rather than invent values. Seed longevity tables trace back to copyrighted Knott's and Splittstoesser books, and the Kew/SER Seed Information Database states no licence ([UNL G2090](https://extensionpubs.unl.edu/publication/g2090/na/html/view); [SER-INSR](https://ser-insr.org/seed-information-database)). Square-foot spacing counts, frost-kill temperatures for vegetables, photoperiod, bulbing and vernalization tables, and stage-wise vegetable nutrient uptake all lacked an open compilation. So did apple and pear chill requirements and home-garden yields. South Africa has no machine-readable registers of agricultural or stock remedies, only Act 36 of 1947 processes ([DALRRD](https://old.dalrrd.gov.za/doaDev/sideMenu/ActNo36_1947/AIC/Guideline%20for%20Registration%20Process%20for%20Agricultural%20Remedies%202015.pdf)). CABI and PPDB content is closed. The pest-treatment ladder should therefore be generic (cultural, mechanical, biological, organic, conventional), keyed to active ingredient and mode-of-action group, and should end with a "check your local registration and label" step. Space allowances for small ruminants, rabbits, quail and waterfowl, THI thresholds for poultry and small ruminants with DOIs, and South African swarm timing were also not found in open sources. Licences that research could not confirm, and so should be treated as closed until checked, include the SAGE, GGCMI and MIRCA crop calendars, the WOFOST parameter repo, Practical Plants, the Measure Your Harvest data, the dst-selector cover-crop data, and the SANBI/SABIF and ARC-PPRI terms.

## Recommended licence, provenance schema and pipeline

**Data licence: CC BY-SA 4.0 for `catalog/`, MIT for code.** This follows the split that OpenStreetMap, Open Food Facts and Wikidata use. CC BY-SA 4.0 accepts CC0, public-domain, CC BY 4.0 and Wikipedia's CC BY-SA 4.0 inputs, and CC 4.0 explicitly covers database rights ([CC wiki](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/4.0/Sui_generis_database_rights)). ODbL would block Wikipedia-derived data, because ODbL and CC BY-SA are incompatible in both directions ([ODbL summary](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/summary/)). CC0 would shut out CC BY data such as ECOCROP, DAD-IS and BBCH. The trade-off is that downstream users must share alike. Recording a licence per value keeps a CC0-only subset exportable later. Ship `catalog/LICENSE`, plus a `NOTICE` file holding the FAO extra terms, the EPPO notice with its download date, and the FAO citation strings. State in the README and wiki that the MIT grant does not cover `catalog/`.

**Provenance schema.** Follow a lightweight Wikidata model (value, qualifiers, references) rather than full PROV-O. A central `catalog/sources.yaml` registry holds the source details once. Each value then points to the registry by ID:

```yaml
# catalog/sources.yaml
- id: fao-ecocrop
  title: ECOCROP
  author: FAO Land and Water Division
  url: https://ecocrop.apps.fao.org/ecocrop/srv/en/home
  license: CC-BY-4.0
  extra_terms: "No commercial promotion; no implied FAO endorsement"
  tos_reviewed: 2026-10-09
  access: manual            # api | bulk-download | html-scrape | manual
# catalog/crops/tomato.yaml
germination_temp_c:
  value: {min: 10, opt: 29, max: 35}
  unit: Cel
  qualifiers: {cultivar: null, region: null}
  evidence: extension-service   # peer-reviewed | government | extension-service | grower-reported | model | traditional
  confidence: high
  rank: preferred               # preferred | normal | deprecated
  sources:
    - {ref: harrington-osu, retrieved: 2026-10-09, locator: "Table 1", snapshot: "sha256:…"}
```

The numbers in this example are placeholders that show the shape, not curated values. Store conflicting values as a range when sources agree on the domain, or as separate qualified statements when they differ by zone or cultivar. Never average them silently, and have CI flag sources whose ranges do not overlap. In the app, show a per-field source popover and a generated "Data sources & licences" page, which matches CC's recommended practice for works with many sources ([CC attribution practices](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/Recommended_practices_for_attribution)).

**Pipeline.** The pipeline runs only on the maintainer side. It runs as a monthly GitHub Actions job and opens a pull request with a reviewable YAML diff. The PR is the audit trail. The steps are:

1. Run one fetcher per source, preferring APIs and dumps: Wikidata SPARQL over plain httpx with a descriptive User-Agent, pygbif, the iNaturalist AWS bucket, GloBI Zenodo dumps, the EPPO bulk exports, and the USDA PLANTS JSON. Use HTML scraping only as a last resort.
2. Save each raw response under its SHA-256 hash, outside git, and record it in a `snapshots.lock`. Raw snapshots may be copyrighted and are kept for verification, not redistribution.
3. Run pure extractors tested against fixture snapshots.
4. Merge, with hand-curated YAML always winning over scraped proposals.
5. Validate: Pydantic or JSON Schema plus `frictionless validate`, with a licence gate that fails CI on any NC, ND or unknown source licence, and a fuzzy-match check that fails when catalog prose matches source text.

The tooling is current as of October 2026: frictionless 5.20.0, pydantic 2.14.0, Protego 0.7.0, selectolax 1.0.0, hishel 1.4.0 and pygbif 0.7.0 ([PyPI frictionless](https://pypi.org/project/frictionless/); [PyPI Protego](https://pypi.org/project/Protego/)). Scrapers must follow these rules:

- Obey robots.txt. It is a convention and not access control, but cached copies expire after 24 hours ([RFC 9309](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309.html)).
- Send about one request every 1–5 seconds per host, using conditional requests.
- Never log in or accept clickwrap terms. hiQ lost on contract after accepting LinkedIn's terms ([ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/alternative-data/hiq-v-linkedin-wrapped-up-web-scraping-lessons-learned/)).
- Record a `tos_reviewed` date for every source.
- Respect iNaturalist's limits: about 1 request per second and 10,000 per day, with a block risk above 5 GB of media per hour ([iNat API practices](https://www.inaturalist.org/pages/api+recommended+practices)).

**Self-hosted instances never scrape.** They only consume tagged catalog releases. This keeps the legal footprint inside one reviewed pipeline and stops thousands of instances from hitting the same sources.

## Conclusion

The bottleneck for CropStack is not finding data. It is earning trust in each value. The open sources that can be bundled are strongest on names, climate envelopes, crop coefficients, phenology codes, breeds and taxonomic links. The practical, gardener-facing knowledge (sowing calendars, spacing, feeding, treatments) sits almost entirely in copyrighted extension and institutional material that can only be cited value by value. CropStack's real differentiator is therefore the evidence label it attaches to each value, more than the size of the catalog. A planner that marks companion pairs "traditional", rotation intervals "disease-specific", yields "commercial benchmark" and pest models "US-calibrated" will be more truthful than any competitor that simply mirrors a single site.

The highest-leverage next steps are relationships rather than code. Ask ARC-VOPI for written permission to restate its South African sowing calendars. Ask FAO whether bulk extraction of ECOCROP is acceptable. Encourage the Measure Your Harvest team to publish under CC BY. Each of these would close a gap that no amount of engineering can. A South African IP lawyer's view on the *Haupt* compilation threshold is the one legal check worth paying for before the first catalog release.
